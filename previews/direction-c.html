2026-08-27 — Anthropic releases the Model Hardware Standard (research preview)

The standard changed on 27 August 2026.

The Model Hardware Standard (MHS) is an open, model-agnostic specification that lets AI agents discover and safely operate lab instruments: liquid handlers, microscopes, robotic arms. Anthropic released it on 27 August 2026 as a research preview; open-sourcing is planned.

A safety envelope, in plain words: the set of limits inside which a device may act — travel, speed, temperature, pressure, duration. We define one for each instrument and enforce it in software and, where it matters, in hardware. That boundary is engineering we do and sign, not a certificate a third party grants. It is what every frame on this page means.

MDK Engineering is independent of Anthropic: no partnership, no preview access, no endorsement. We build for the MHS era and are applying to the research preview. The standard lets an agent operate the instrument; our work is making it understand the experiment, and deciding what it may never do to the device.

Flagship proof — University of Oxford

A robotic system that handles ~10 µm diamond particles.

Built at the University of Oxford for optical quantum computing. At ten micrometres, adhesion beats gravity: a particle clings to whatever touches it, and macro-scale intuition stops working. The rig combines microscope optics, precision stages and learned control to pick, move and place single diamond particles.
